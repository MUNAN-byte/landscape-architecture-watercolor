# Prompt Recipes

Use these as scaffolds, not fixed aesthetic presets. Fill every bracket from observed source facts. The source photo supplies all content and color.

## Shared master prompt

```text
Create a clean vertical editorial diptych from the supplied photograph.

Upper section: preserve the source photograph faithfully. Allow only proportional scaling or a restrained crop. No redraw, relighting, retouching, extension, filter, vignette, frame, shadow, or invented content.

Lower section: warm ivory cold-press watercolor paper. Reconstruct the photograph as an abstract visual memory, not a miniature painting. First classify subject, camera distance, and dominant light. Select no more than five cue types. Use one primary mark family and no more than two supporting families. Every mark must correspond to an observed mass, axis, rhythm, light, reflection, color role, or negative space.

Watercolor behavior: transparent washes, reserved paper white, selective hard and soft edges, natural pigment pooling, lost edges, and purposeful brush direction. Texture alone is not watercolor. Use splashes or drips only when the source supports them.

Keep roughly 65–80% of the panel clean or nearly clean. Add one source-grounded English title of two to five words in restrained editorial serif typography.

Avoid: complete illustration, photo filter, traced facade, equal detail everywhere, invented colors, decorative splatter, extra text, logo, watermark, vignette, black corners, frame, shadow, collage, or mockup effects.
```

## Storefront or illuminated interior

```text
Classify as [near/mid] architecture with artificial light. Preserve [2–4 identity cues: frame/opening/display mass/repeated lamps/sign trajectory]. Translate frames into restrained dark vertical washes, lamps into paper-white light points, the dominant display color into overlapping geometric blocks, and visible writing into non-legible calligraphic trajectories. Do not reproduce incidental text or draw the room/shop literally. Primary family: geometric washes. Supporting families: structural strokes and calligraphic/light-point marks.
```

## Iconic colorful facade

```text
Classify as a near-to-mid iconic facade in [light direction]. Keep at most five cue types: [distinctive roof/one tower or opening/a sparse repeated rhythm/a concentrated color-memory wash/one foliage or context mass]. Remove the complete facade outline. Repeated windows or balconies must become uneven floating marks rather than floors. Compress ornament or mosaic into a limited cluster of transparent blooms; never render it piece by piece.
```

## Wide lake, coast, or mountain landscape

```text
Classify as a far landscape with strong horizontal depth. Reduce space to two or three broken atmospheric bands. Keep one dark or warm anchor and one directional rhythm from the foreground. Boats, paths, poles, or trees should become tapered strokes or grouped masses, not complete objects. Use value, temperature, overlap, and lost edges for depth; do not outline mountains or repeat decorative wave symbols.
```

## Monumental vertical landmark

```text
Classify as a frontal or near-frontal landmark with strong vertical rhythm. Preserve one simplified stepped mass, [number] expressive vertical needle/tower strokes, [number] dark opening marks, one distinctive void, and a low scale rhythm if people are present. People are continuous tapered dashes with no separate heads or limbs. Dissolve the mass edges into paper so it does not become a full facade illustration.
```

## Quality-correction prompts

### Too literal

```text
The lower panel is too literal. Keep the photographic section unchanged. Replace only the panel. Delete the complete scene/facade/object outlines. Retain no more than five cue types and express repeated objects as one mark family. Increase untouched paper and make recognition depend on relationships, not depiction.
```

### Watercolor filter look

```text
The panel looks like a digital image with watercolor texture. Rebuild form from transparent overlapping washes, reserved paper white, pigment pooling, and selective hard/soft edges. Remove smooth gradients, photographic micro-detail, uniform grain, and outlined forms.
```

### Decorative effects

```text
Remove every splash, stain, drip, or line that cannot be traced to source light, reflection, weather, foliage, writing, or structural rhythm. Keep at most one justified incidental effect family.
```
